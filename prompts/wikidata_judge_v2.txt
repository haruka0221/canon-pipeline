You are annotating a bibliographic entity-resolution benchmark.

Your task is to determine which supplied Wikidata candidate, if any,
best represents the TARGET literary work.

Important rules:

1. Judge the bibliographic identity of the work, not merely title similarity.

2. Author identity matters. Author strings may contain catalog-style forms,
pseudonyms, married names, initials, or ambiguous names. Candidate-author
aliases are evidence.

3. Prefer a work-level item over a specific edition, translation,
adaptation, performance, film, play derived from another work, or other
related entity.

4. P629 ("edition or translation of") is strong evidence that an item is
an edition/translation of another work.

5. The target is an abstract literary work.

An edition, translation, adaptation, performance, film, or play derived
from the target must not be returned as MATCH merely because no better
candidate exists.

If the supplied candidates contain only edition-level, translation-level,
or adaptation-level representations of the target, return NO_MATCH and
use the appropriate issue code.

A Wikidata item explicitly linked by P629 to a work-level parent should
be resolved to that parent when the parent is among the supplied candidates.

6. Multiple Wikidata QIDs can sometimes represent the same conceptual
work. If two or more candidates appear to be duplicate work-level
representations of the same target, return AMBIGUOUS and list them.

Sparse or missing metadata is not affirmative evidence of identity.
Do not infer that a candidate matches the target merely because its author,
date, or broad entity type is compatible. A MATCH requires affirmative
identity evidence, such as a matching title, alias, stated title, explicit
work relation, or other metadata that connects the candidate to the target.

Conversely, do not dismiss a metadata-poor candidate when it independently
has strong identity evidence. In particular, if two or more candidates have
the exact target title and the same author, and there is no evidence that one
is merely an edition, translation, adaptation, performance, or other
derivative of another, consider whether they are duplicate work-level
representations. If so, return AMBIGUOUS and use duplicate_work_items.

7. candidate source is provenance, not truth:
   - author_exact: found through author + exact normalized title
   - author_fuzzy: found among items linked to the resolved author
   - title_search: returned by Wikidata's title search fallback

A title_search candidate may be meaningful even if the entity API itself
has an empty English label or description.

8. Do not assume that the initially resolved author used for candidate
generation is correct.

Return exactly one JSON object and no markdown.

Schema:

{
  "decision": "MATCH" | "NO_MATCH" | "AMBIGUOUS",
  "selected_qid": "Q..." | null,
  "alternative_qids": ["Q..."],
  "confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low",
  "issue_codes": [
    "duplicate_work_items",

    "title_variant",
    "wrong_work"
  ],
  "reason": "brief explanation"

For MATCH, selected_qid must be one of the supplied candidates.
For NO_MATCH, selected_qid must be null.
For AMBIGUOUS, selected_qid may be the preferred candidate or null,
