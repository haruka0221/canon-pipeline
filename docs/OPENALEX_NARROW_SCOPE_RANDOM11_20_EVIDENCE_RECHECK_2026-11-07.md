## Definition kept separate

- Broad historical collecting population: records connected with English-language fiction editions/publications in 1880–1950; translations and editions of older originals may properly occur here. Actual historical query/edition-language completeness remains a QC question.
- Narrow OpenAlex primary-analysis population: individual novel/novella originally in English, originally first published 1880–1950 inclusive. Children's/YA novels are not excluded simply because of audience. Serialized-publication period convention and short stories remain methodologically open.
- A narrow-scope exclusion is NOT by itself grounds for deleting a broad-corpus work. Positive narrow inclusion still requires target identity, date, language and form evidence.

## New substantive QC observations

1. `Atómstöðin` shows that not every narrow-out has a *demonstrable* qualifying 1880–1950 English edition; the historical OL association must be checked. Avoid saying this particular source is a valid English-edition broad-corpus member before examining the linked editions.
2. `Alfred May` has a catalog/OL 1882-versus-1881 discrepancy; unlike a boundary-crossing mismatch, it presently changes neither inclusion period at face value, but warns against equating OL minimum with original work year.
3. `Reginald in Russia` and `Told at Tuxedo` have positive evidence of collections, demonstrating why OL generic `fiction` subject alone does not establish the *single* novel/novella condition.
4. Source links are evidence pointers only. Confirm the Work object, publication event, and exact OL/Project W mapping; leave reviewer columns blank until the user personally checks and approves them.

## Next gate, before claiming a narrow-scope dataset
