## Broader methodological findings

1. **Work/Edition alias:** `Torment` (1951) versus `Better Angel` (1933) shows why original-work dating and OpenAlex query-title recall require separate checks. If the search used only `Torment`, low candidate count could reflect query coverage rather than neglected scholarship on the original 1933 novel.
2. **Corpus contamination distinct from justified broad inclusion:** Norfolk's `Finding List` is nonfiction bibliography. By contrast, a translated 1892 edition can be expected within a broad corpus of English-language manifestations. Both still require target-level checks before formal treatment.
3. **Multiple identity risk:** The three OL IDs for `Snow Treasure` are not independently three different novels, but neither may they be silently merged. Preserve Project W and OL IDs unchanged.
4. **Collection evidence is not a genre label:** The 1904 Eastman volume has folklore and descriptive material as well as separately narrated tales. The 1916 Wyndham volume has previously published episodes, but whether those form one novel or an author's collection remains a literary-form decision.
5. Original source review and human scope adjudication remain separate. All 80 researcher fields remain blank and all 32,066 active W plus OpenAlex v3 pair counts are unchanged.
